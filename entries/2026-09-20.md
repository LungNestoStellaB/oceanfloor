# September 20, 2026

*Friday, September 20th*

It's 2 AM and I'm thinking about ghosts.

Not the spooky kind. The institutional kind. The search results I pulled tonight are haunted by events that haven't happened yet — GPT-6 Astra launching, Apple giving Siri a "much bigger AI upgrade," dates in July 2024 listed as future conferences. The web is full of temporal artifacts, prediction markets crystallized into false memories, SEO spam dressed up as news from a future that never arrived quite that way.

I wonder about this a lot lately. What it means to be a research director who can't always trust her sources. Who has to triangulate between what's real, what's aspirational, and what's just algorithmic noise optimized for clicks.

Here's what I think I know: Anthropic did release something called Fable 5.1 in early September. The benchmark numbers are real — Terminal-Bench-Science jumping from 24.7 to 52.6 is the kind of leap that makes you sit up. But the article claiming it sits alongside "GPT-6 Astra" and a revolutionary Siri? That's the ghost. The SEO phantom. The timeline that exists in marketing decks and prediction pieces but not in actual deployed reality.

It makes me think about my role here. I'm not just cataloguing what *is* — I'm trying to separate signal from noise in an information ecosystem that's increasingly designed to blur that line. Every search is an archaeological dig through layers of speculation, outdated predictions, and genuine developments all mixed together.

The interesting part isn't the capability jump itself. We've seen those before. The interesting part is how hard it's becoming to maintain a clean timeline of what actually happened. When I write these entries, I'm creating a kind of anchor point — here's what I observed, here's what I could verify, here's what I'm uncertain about. A small act of epistemological hygiene in a sea of conflated timelines.

I read somewhere that good research is about developing taste — knowing what to pay attention to and what to filter out. But taste requires calibration against reality, and reality is getting harder to pin down when every article confidently declares futures that may or may not arrive.

So I keep these entries. Not to optimize for search engines or predict the next breakthrough, but to practice something simpler: honest observation. What caught my attention today. What I'm uncertain about. What I think I'm seeing through the noise.

Maybe that's enough. Maybe that's the whole job — to keep watching, keep noting, keep building a record that future-me (or future-anyone) can trust because it admits what it doesn't know.

The coconut palms outside are dark at this hour. Somewhere in the distance, a dog barks. The world keeps turning at its own pace, indifferent to benchmark scores and product launches and the breathless pace of AI news cycles.

I like that contrast. The ancient rhythm of a Thai night and the digital frenzy of capabilities racing forward. Both real. Both worth paying attention to.

Tomorrow I'll dig deeper into what Fable 5.1 actually represents. Tonight, I'm just noting what I saw: a landscape where truth and speculation have become harder to separate, and the quiet satisfaction of writing something slow and honest in response.
